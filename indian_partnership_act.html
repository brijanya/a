Basic General Content

Indian Partnership Act 1932

Important sections of Indian Contracts Act 1872

  1. Section 4 : Defines "partnership" as the relation between persons who have agreed to share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.
  2. Section 5 : States that the relation of partnership arises from contract and not from status (such as inheritance or birth).
  3. Section 6 : Provides the guidelines for determining the existence of a partnership (the "test of partnership"), emphasizing mutual agency as the real test.
  4. Section 9: Imposes a general duty on partners to carry on the business to the greatest common advantage, to be just and faithful, and to render true accounts.
  5. Section 10: Requires partners to indemnify the firm for any loss caused by their fraud in the conduct of the business.
  6. Section 12: Outlines the rights of partners to take part in the conduct of the business and have access to books.
  7. Section 13: Details rules regarding partners' remuneration, right to share profits equally, and interest on capital or advances (unless modified by contract).
  8. Section 14: Defines the "property of the firm" and specifies that all property acquired for the firm belongs to it.
  9. Section 18: Establishes that a partner is the agent of the firm for the purposes of the business (the principle of mutual agency).
  10. Section 19: Defines the scope of a partner's implied authority to bind the firm through normal business acts.
  11. Section 25: Establishes the joint and several unlimited liability of all partners for all acts of the firm done while they are partners.
  12. Section 28: Imposes liability on a person who holds out (by words or conduct) as a partner, making them liable as a partner to third parties (Doctrine of Holding Out).
  13. Section 31: States that no new partner can be introduced into a firm without the consent of all existing partners.
  14. Section 32: Governs the conditions and liability of a partner retiring from the firm.
  15. Section 34: Outlines the legal grounds and process for the expulsion of a partner by a majority.
  16. Section 35: Details the effect of a partner's death on the firm and the estate's liability.
  17. Section 37: Grants an outgoing partner (or their estate) the right to share in post-dissolution profits or claim interest until final settlement.
  18. Section 39: Defines the dissolution of a partnership between all the partners of a firm as the dissolution of the firm itself.
  19. Section 40 - 44: Specify the various modes of dissolution—by agreement, compulsory dissolution, on the happening of certain contingencies, by notice of partnership at will, and dissolution by the court.
  20. Section 45: Imposes liability for acts done by partners after dissolution until public notice of the dissolution is given.
  21. Section 58: Prescribes the procedure for the registration of a firm with the Registrar of Firms.
  22. Section 69: Details the severe legal consequences of non-registration, notably barring unregistered firms and their partners from enforcing contractual rights in courts.
